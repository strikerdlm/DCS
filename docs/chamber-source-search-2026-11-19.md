# Public hypobaric chamber outcomes and ADRAC source audit

19 November 2026

## GitHub data and ADRAC

The GitHub data can support **emulation of recorded ADRAC outputs**. The `DCS_Risk_DB_2025.csv` archive in the DCS repository describes its targets as calculator queries, in percent. TinyDCS already fits a log-logistic approximation and a machine-learning surrogate to the cleaned grid. The companion DCS-model-with-Machine-Learning repository has a separate workbook experiment. Its released data card identifies probability labels with an unverified generator and no source exposure outcomes, risk sets or follow-up. The companion also has a clinical NASA Table F1 transcription that is already represented in the current chamber database. [DCS grid data description](../legacy/Model_Rel_Candidate/README.md) · [Companion workbook data card](https://github.com/strikerdlm/DCS-model-with-Machine-Learning/blob/2e2292ec25e969165d59333d588b4f416629debf/artifacts/data-card.md) · [Chamber benchmark](chamber-benchmark.md)

I compared the primary workbook at companion commit `2e2292ec25e969165d59333d588b4f416629debf` with the raw archive. The exact join used altitude in feet, prebreathe minutes, exercise category and time at altitude in minutes, with the workbook labels converted from fractions to percent. There were 2,304 distinct workbook settings, 15,908 distinct grid settings and 2,234 settings in common. At the common settings, 867 labels were identical and 1,367 differed. The difference was at least one percentage point for 1,305 settings. The workbook SHA-256 was `ce07d3b6fd5d922cc814509ea1928152438c29194aa8c724ea4b7f3f42cde906`. The raw CSV SHA-256 was `8d1d7314e2db635928941f00ed3031b861c0b88355a166f7108ae3dbf59f4e59`. Exact matches corroborate shared numbers in parts of the archives. The discrepancies leave version identity and workbook provenance unresolved.

The 2004 AFRL paper is the primary source for reconstructing the original model. It reports a stratified log-logistic fit, an exercise–pressure interaction and a separate bubble-growth calculation that contributes to the combined prediction. Table I gives statistical coefficients and scales. The Appendix A transcription requires reconciliation before numerical reproduction. The paper describes pressure in mmHg, but direct substitution of the published pressures in Eq. 7 with the printed coefficient magnitudes does not provide a plausible benchmark match. The exponent convention in Eq. 6 also needs to be tied to the SAS scale and to the increasing cumulative-incidence curves. The combined algorithm needs its pressure/exercise encoding, bubble-to-risk implementation and a reference for the target calculator version. The historic AFRL exposure-level onset and censoring records would be required to refit the original survival analysis. [Pilmanis et al., pp. 753–758](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/75/9/article-p749.pdf) · [AFRL development report](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA397485.pdf)

Kannan and Raychaudhuri's 1998 Armstrong Laboratory report describes how the team joined the original two files on man-flight number. Its marginal Tables 3.1 and 3.4 give 518 DCS events and 908 censored observations across 1,426 exposures. The prose says 906 censored observations. The report also gives earlier exercise codes, alternative coefficients and median onset benchmarks. Webb's 2010 USAFSAM database manual documents an Access database of more than 3,000 subject-exposures and describes how its Subject, Flight, Segment, Symptoms, Bubbles and Study Design tables link. It identifies the ADRAC validation study as `MV` and describes the database file as agency-hosted. The public manual supplies the acquisition crosswalk for requesting actual linked records. A public download of the Access file was unavailable in this search. [1998 survival report](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA338089.pdf) · [USAFSAM database manual](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA521198.pdf)

## AFRL validation profiles and overlap

The prospective study had five profiles, each with 30 exposures. Volunteers completed two profiles each. The 2004 paper publishes model predictions, observed Kaplan–Meier DCS estimates with CI margins and any-VGE estimates. NEDU Appendix E tabulates integer outcomes for the same named protocols. Its MV-A, MV-B and MV-E rows each have 31 exposures. The overlap holds whether denominators change by version or the same observations are reported at different horizons. The two exact settings shared by the 2025 grid and the paper had grid predictions of 75% and 45%, while the paper gives model predictions of 53% and 44%. Profile A is called moderate in Table II and mild in Table III. These comparisons retain source wording and raw values.
