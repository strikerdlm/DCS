# International chamber evidence, release 1.18

Release 1.239 adds 12 cause percentages and the conference's observed interruption tally for the existing Taiwan cohort. Among the 57 recorded noncompletions, the conference attributes 15.8% to severe hypoxia and the journal attributes 42.1% to severe hypoxia. The publications group pressure-related causes differently. The conference reports middle-ear block and sinus pain individually for the 592 interrupted completers. The journal reports a broader pressure-related category for its 572 interrupted completers. Exact counts for each cause and a clinical DCS numerator remain unavailable. The journal's 572 interruptions imply 3.1% to one decimal place with either the full cohort or the derived number of completers. The journal prints 3.2%. Both source tallies remain in the paired databases under one study. Release 1.18 records ten publications and four observations. The search ledger includes discovery, extraction and primary-source verification. [Sources](../data/chamber/v1.18/sources.csv) · [Observations](../data/chamber/v1.18/observations.csv) · [Search runs](../data/chamber/v1.18/search_runs.csv) · [Search hits](../data/chamber/v1.18/search_hits.csv)

- **Peng et al., China, 2016.** The official Chinese author abstract reports **0/17 participants with DCS** during simulated extravehicular activity at 7,200 m. The 17 men were selected using a susceptibility assessment and completed 30 minutes of oxygen denitrogenation at 3,000 m. The abstract mentions Grade 1–2 precordial bubble signals without a participant tally. Exposure duration and delayed follow-up are unavailable. [Official author abstract](https://www.cmse.gov.cn/dmt/cbw/zrht/2016n/2016ndsq/201609/t20160929_40527.html)
- **Lee et al., Taiwan, 2021.** The center conducted 1,685 chamber sessions and recorded **18,487 trainee-exposures** from January 2001 through August 2018. The 2019 conference and 2021 journal abstracts agree on **57 all-cause noncompletions**. The conference abstract reports **592 interruptions** and the journal reports **572**. Both print **3.2%**. The earlier numerator reproduces the printed percentage over 18,487. The journal's denominator and the reason for the revision remain unresolved. The DCS-specific breakdown and delayed follow-up are unavailable in both abstracts. [Lee et al., journal abstract](https://www.airitilibrary.com/Article/Detail/16839595-202112-202209020003-202209020003-42-54) · [2019 conference proceedings, abstract A-I-003](https://wwwndmc.ndmutsgh.edu.tw/files/web/191/file_up/10000/6512/%E5%9C%8B%E9%98%B2%E9%86%AB%E5%AD%B8%E9%99%A2%E5%BB%BA%E6%A0%A1118%E5%B9%B4%E6%A0%A1%E6%85%B6%E5%A4%A7%E6%9C%83%E6%9A%A8%E7%AC%AC46%E5%B1%86%E5%9C%8B%E8%BB%8D%E8%BB%8D%E9%86%AB%E5%AD%B8%E8%A1%93%E7%A0%94%E8%A8%8E%E6%9C%83-%E5%B0%88%E5%88%8A-%E6%92%A4%E7%A8%BF2nd%E7%AF%87%E5%BE%8C.pdf)
- **Kutz et al., United States, 2023.** Table III Case 15 describes one referral following a suited chamber exposure and a subsequent T-38 flight. All four reviewers favored DCS. The source reports onset more than an hour after return to ground and symptoms during the following flight. The study sampled referrals and provides no chamber-exposure denominator. [Kutz et al., Table III](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/amhp/94/1/article-p11.pdf#page=5)
- **Gibbons and Pilmanis, United States, 2003.** The aggregate paper explicitly links the only combined pulmonary and CNS case in its fast-ascent arm to the separate Gibbons case report. The source registry retains the published VGE and symptom times and links them to the existing arm. The new release adds no count for this case. [Aggregate Results and reference 6](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/74/10/article-p1052.pdf) · [Original case report](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/74/6/article-p675.pdf)
