## Release 1.242

Seven selected RAF chamber histories and 26 clinical milestones were extracted from Fryer's report. They include reported chokes, skin manifestations, delayed migraine-like and neurological symptoms, two hospital evaluations with plasma-volume measurements, and a case with documented post-descent hyperventilation. Case 2's introduction says in-flight, while the numbered narrative explicitly describes a chamber. Case 16 describes transient difficulty initiating voluntary movements during descent, clarified at a later interview. In Case 25, Fryer describes bends pain before descent and later reports end-tidal CO₂ of 18 mmHg during a hyperventilation attack. The timings preserve their distinct starting points. ## Release 1.241

Fryer describes separate RAF neurological chamber episodes in 1941 and 1957. The earlier case developed new visual and motor symptoms approximately an hour after descent. In the later case, two source observers estimated 30 seconds and two minutes for the same interval at 37,000 ft. Both estimates and the following day's clinical events retain their original time reference. These are selected case histories without at-risk denominators. Fryer attributes the 1941 episode to Roxburgh. The accessible 1953 journal citation matches the author and year, with the identity of the original account still unverified. ## Release 1.240

The Dutch full report observed six reactions with bradycardia in 120 repeated hypoxic exposures across 30 volunteers. Two of those exposures involved syncope with asystole. The earlier conference abstract reports the same site, altitude, participant count, four-session design and two asystole cases. The counts are represented once, with likely overlap recorded. The full report also gives a separate five-person crossover pilot with syncope or near-syncope in 5 of 5 ANP exposures and 0 of 5 placebo exposures. Its relationship to the 11-person main study is unresolved. The extraction records hypoxic adverse events for differential diagnosis. Clinical DCS counts and delayed DCS surveillance are unavailable in these results. ## Release 1.239

Release 1.239 adds 12 cause percentages and the conference's observed interruption tally for the existing Taiwan cohort. Among the 57 recorded noncompletions, the conference attributes 15.8% to severe hypoxia and the journal attributes 42.1% to severe hypoxia. The publications group pressure-related causes differently. The conference reports middle-ear block and sinus pain individually for the 592 interrupted completers. The journal reports a broader pressure-related category for its 572 interrupted completers. Exact counts for each cause and a clinical DCS numerator remain unavailable. The journal's 572 interruptions imply 3.1% to one decimal place with either the full cohort or the derived number of completers. The journal prints 3.2%. Both source tallies remain in the paired databases under one study.
