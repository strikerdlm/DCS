# 1992 HDSD Table III

Conkin, Bedahl and Van Liew document a historical database of 378 groups and 130,012 person-exposures. The report said the full dataset was available on request in 1992. NASA's record has no downloadable data file. Tavily found the publisher's six-page article, and the page image was used to verify the extraction. The article's PDF remains at the publisher. [Article PDF](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/63/9/article-p819.pdf) · [NASA record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19920072110)

Table III (p. 821) prints 27 groups of at least 200 person-exposures. The extracted denominators total 117,422, matching the table. `literature_hdsd_compilation` preserves the HDSD report number, duration, absolute pressure, exercise flag, and the reported percentages for any DCS and serious DCS. Blanks in either outcome column represent the source's missing-value marker. The source definition includes chokes in serious DCS and excludes isolated paresthesia from any DCS. The outcome groups can overlap. Exact event counts cannot be recovered from these rounded figures. [Table III and endpoint definitions](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/63/9/article-p819.pdf#page=3)

Rows 325–328 reproduce the denominators and the two corresponding rates for the four Motley et al. training groups already sourced in v1.11. Those rows total 68,422 exposure entries. For the staged flights, the HDSD pressure and time are printed as 4.4 psia and one hour. The original Motley protocols give the subsequent 38,000-ft stage, which is the source used for trajectory information. All 27 HDSD rows remain on overlap hold. Matching group sizes alone cannot establish independence or an exact link to another historical report. ## Archive coverage and later reuse

Tables I and II reconcile 80 sources and 378 group records covering 130,012 person-exposures. The 376 dated records include 129,645 male and 321 female exposures. Two undated records account for 46 additional male exposures. The bands represent publication dates. Some participants were exposed more than once. [Original tables](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/63/9/article-p819.pdf#page=2) · [Coverage extract](../data/chamber/v1.235/publication_bands.csv)

The 2001 NASA analysis selected 258 tests from a later 456-test HDSD release. Twelve Table III rows have counterparts or candidates in Appendix C. The source reports corroborate eight group links. Four remain candidates because group identifiers, denominators or severity rates differ. Smedal's pain requiring descent and the compilations' serious DCS remain distinct endpoints. The 1996 Van Liew paper separately identifies its analysis as a subset of the 1994 forced-descent data with known ascent rates. [NASA 2001 report](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/conkin-dcs-estimation-tp210196-2001.pdf#page=9) · [Group crosswalk](../data/chamber/v1.235/serious_group_links.csv) · [Van Liew follow-up](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040173090)

HDSD record 77 and Gray and Masland's Table I give the same 429-flight denominator for a two-hour resting exposure near 35,000 ft. The HDSD labels 2.3% as serious DCS. Gray and Masland print 2.3% for gas-pain descent, 0.93% for descent with both bends and chokes, and 1.6% for chokes alone. The reports retain their published labels, and the group remains on overlap hold. Release 1.236 also records the original gas-pain and other-cause percentages. The two flight groups that appear in both original tables each retain a single zero for severe gas pains with both source locators.
