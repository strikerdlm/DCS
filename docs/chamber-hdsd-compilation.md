# 1992 HDSD Table III

Conkin, Bedahl and Van Liew document a historical database of 378 groups and 130,012 person-exposures. The report said the full dataset was available on request in 1992. NASA's record has no downloadable data file. Tavily found the publisher's six-page article, and the page image was used to verify the extraction. The article's PDF remains at the publisher. [Article PDF](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/63/9/article-p819.pdf) · [NASA record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19920072110)

Table III (p. 821) prints 27 groups of at least 200 person-exposures. The extracted denominators total 117,422, matching the table. `literature_hdsd_compilation` preserves the HDSD report number, duration, absolute pressure, exercise flag, and the reported percentages for any DCS and serious DCS. Blanks in either outcome column represent the source's missing-value marker. The source definition includes chokes in serious DCS and excludes isolated paresthesia from any DCS. The outcome groups can overlap. Exact event counts cannot be recovered from these rounded figures. [Table III and endpoint definitions](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/63/9/article-p819.pdf#page=3)

Rows 325–328 reproduce the denominators and the two corresponding rates for the four Motley et al. training groups already sourced in v1.11. Those rows total 68,422 exposure entries. For the staged flights, the HDSD pressure and time are printed as 4.4 psia and one hour. The original Motley protocols give the subsequent 38,000-ft stage, which is the source used for trajectory information. All 27 HDSD rows remain on overlap hold. Matching group sizes alone cannot establish independence or an exact link to another historical report. ## Archive coverage and later reuse

Tables I and II reconcile 80 sources and 378 group records covering 130,012 person-exposures. The 376 dated records include 129,645 male and 321 female exposures. Two undated records account for 46 additional male exposures. The bands represent publication dates. Some participants were exposed more than once. [Original tables](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/63/9/article-p819.pdf#page=2) · [Coverage extract](../data/chamber/v1.235/publication_bands.csv)

The 2001 NASA analysis selected 258 tests from a later 456-test HDSD release. Twelve Table III rows have counterparts or candidates in Appendix C. The source reports corroborate eight group links. Four remain candidates because group identifiers, denominators or severity rates differ. Smedal's pain requiring descent and the compilations' serious DCS remain distinct endpoints. The 1996 Van Liew paper separately identifies its analysis as a subset of the 1994 forced-descent data with known ascent rates. [NASA 2001 report](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/conkin-dcs-estimation-tp210196-2001.pdf#page=9) · [Group crosswalk](../data/chamber/v1.235/serious_group_links.csv) · [Van Liew follow-up](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040173090)

HDSD record 77 and Gray and Masland's Table I give the same 429-flight denominator for a two-hour resting exposure near 35,000 ft. The HDSD labels 2.3% as serious DCS. Gray and Masland print 2.3% for gas-pain descent, 0.93% for descent with both bends and chokes, and 1.6% for chokes alone. The reports retain their published labels, and the group remains on overlap hold. Release 1.236 also records the original gas-pain and other-cause percentages. The two flight groups that appear in both original tables each retain a single zero for severe gas pains with both source locators. [Gray and Masland tables](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/joam/17/5/article-p483.pdf#page=2) · [Primary outcome crosswalk](../data/chamber/v1.236/primary_rate_links.csv)

## Original Army Air Forces reports

Three project reports add 160 exact count rows and 160 printed percentages to the paired release. The counts include nested symptom, descent, severity and circulatory categories. Each observation retains its source table, group and counting unit. The archive scans were checked against their text transcriptions. [Versioned extracts](../data/chamber/v1.237) · [Retrieval and source record](../data/chamber/publication-review/retrievals-2026-11-22-gray-sam-project-reports.json)

- **Project 248-1** reports 508 flights at 38,000 ft in three equipment groups. The three arm denominators are 239, 48 and 221. Aeroembolism descents number 39, 10 and 48, respectively. The trained and untrained arms in Tables IV–VI are nested within those reports. For the 145-person subgroup, Table VI labels the pressure as six inches of water while Tables IV–V and Methods give eight inches. Table VI also prints 2.4% beside one circulatory reaction preceded by chokes. The extract retains the pressure and percentage discrepancies. Table VII gives 11 descents under its decompression sickness label in the 43 trained flights. Table IV separately gives eight aeroembolism and three trapped-gas descents there. [Table I](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015073997747&seq=10) · [Table IV](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015073997747&seq=13) · [Table VI](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015073997747&seq=15) · [Table VII](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015073997747&seq=16)

- **Project 252-1** gives five mild bends and nine severe descents with circulatory reactions in 62 cadets at 45,000 ft. The nine include five with severe gas pains and four with a primary circulatory reaction. The published denominator includes four cadets from a flight stopped at eight minutes. NASA Appendix C p. 43 row 22 gives zero serious DCS cases in the same 62-exposure group and cites the work as CAM 315. The adjacent zero-of-62 row cites Project 458 and has uncertain linkage. Project 248's 42-cadet comparison also remains on overlap hold. Project 252 separately cites 3.4% bends-or-chokes descents in 1,055 historical flights over the first 30 minutes at 38,000 ft. The exact numerator is unavailable. [Methods](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015073997762&seq=4) · [Table I](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015073997762&seq=8) · [Historical comparator](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015073997762&seq=5) · [NASA Appendix C](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/conkin-dcs-estimation-tp210196-2001.pdf#page=49)

- **Project 409-1** gives two 204-cadet cohorts at 38,000 ft, breathing either an oxygen–CO₂ mixture or oxygen. Each reports 27 forced descents for bends and seven for chokes. The period and counts do not establish an identity with either of the 204-exposure HDSD rows. The published grade and outcome groupings remain separate. [Methods](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015073998141&seq=3) · [Table I](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015073998141&seq=5)

- **Project 458-1** has a confirmed title and report number. Two Air Force subject indexes list January 1946. NASA TM-100472 cites October 3, 1944, and NASA TP-210196 cites 1944. The original remains unavailable and the release preserves each date and locator. The indexes and the National Library of Medicine catalogue document possible retrieval routes. The database retains the candidate HDSD links to Project 458 and CAM 137. [Air Force index](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015074026637&seq=43) · [NASA TM-100472](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19890012936/downloads/19890012936.pdf#page=26) · [NASA TP-210196](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/conkin-dcs-estimation-tp210196-2001.pdf)

The earlier transcription of Gray, Mahady and Masland's 1946 Table II identifies a 204-exposure group after 15 minutes of ground oxygen with 0.5% bends-with-chokes and 1.5% chokes-only descents. The two published percentages align at their reported precision with HDSD row 90's 2.0% serious DCS, and the report's 950-flight control comparison matches the denominator and two-hour 38,000-ft profile of HDSD row 93. Both remain candidate links pending the original HDSD group identifiers.
