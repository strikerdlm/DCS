# FAA CAMI oxygen-mask experiment safety evidence

Campbell and Jay's September 2025 technical report DOT/FAA/AM-25/52 evaluates passenger oxygen-mask performance in a research hypobaric chamber. The full-text reassessment uses the existing OpenAlex report identity and retains the original search-hit disposition. The DCS discussion adds supplementary research safety evidence. The study population falls outside the operational chamber staff-versus-trainee comparison. [Report documentation and DOI](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/86854/dot_86854_DS1.pdf#page=3) · [Original search hit](../data/chamber/v1.8/search_hits.csv) · [Reassessment record](../data/chamber/publication-review/retrievals-2026-11-20-faa-ofos-safety.json)

## Exposure counts and aborts

The authors recruited **14 participants** and report **16 testing sessions**, **four aborts**, and **12 completed profiles**. One participant and one inside observer occupied the chamber per flight. This yields **12 completed person-profile exposures per role**, derived from the completed profile count and occupancy. Some observers repeated and the number of unique observers is unavailable. The 16 sessions include aborted attempts. Completed profiles describe protocol completion, and the report shows variation in time spent at the highest altitudes. [Occupancy, printed p. 6](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/86854/dot_86854_DS1.pdf#page=13) · [Repeat visits and Results, pp. 18 and 20](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/86854/dot_86854_DS1.pdf#page=25) · [Excursion, p. 27](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/86854/dot_86854_DS1.pdf#page=34)

Two ear-block participants did not return. Intestinal gas-expansion pain caused one abort on ascent, and an aviator-mask seal failure caused the other. Those two participants later completed the profile on rescheduled visits. The methods place mask-seal verification before proceeding, while the Results leave the actual failed attempt's phase and altitude unspecified. The exact stage of the ear blocks and the pressure histories of the aborted attempts are also unavailable. [Mask fit, p. 12](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/86854/dot_86854_DS1.pdf#page=19) · [Aborts and rescheduling, p. 20](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/86854/dot_86854_DS1.pdf#page=27)

## Outcome and follow-up

The report describes **zero reported DCS signs or symptoms in either role** in flight, immediately after flight and through 24 hours. Both roles underwent active monitoring during flight and for one hour after return to ground level. After release, the report describes instructions to report symptoms and seek care through 24 hours. Follow-up completeness after release is unknown. The safety narrative supplies no completion-stratified outcomes or session-level follow-up data. The two role-specific safety zeros retain null outcome denominators, with the derived completed exposure counts recorded separately.
