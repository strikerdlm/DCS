# 2026 AsMA chamber case and related reports

Mendelsohn and Sanders describe one familiarization chamber flight that reached 25,000 ft after 45 minutes of prebreathe. The participant removed the mask at altitude for approximately four minutes. She reported right elbow and wrist symptoms during the flight and presented for evaluation about one hour after returning to ground. The exact onset time, total dwell time, gas delivery, event date and facility are unavailable. [AsMA abstract 223, PDF pp. 232–233](https://asma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/AsMA_Program_Book_edited_cleanup_draft7-1.pdf#page=232)

The authors interpreted the presentation as DCS. Symptoms persisted through the first reported treatment check at 20 minutes, with improvement around 60 minutes into treatment and eventual resolution. The six milestones preserve the separate time origins for mask replacement, clinical assessment and treatment response. The existing EBSCO citation, public journal record and programme text share one report ID and one indexed case. The abstract supplies neither a risk set nor recurrence follow-up. The 2024 NASA training summary lacks the dates and case-level details needed to adjudicate possible overlap, so this observation remains outside pooled counts and model fitting. [Journal abstract](https://openurl.ebsco.com/contentitem/gcd:197020995?id=ebsco%3Agcd%3A197020995&crl=c&jrnl=23756314) · [NASA 2024 summary, section 6.0(a)](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/pfo-dcs-summary-report-august-2024.pdf)

The EA-6 conference abstract and NASA presentation describe the same 16-person study, with two Type II cases in the 120-minute prebreathe arm and one Type I case in the 150-minute arm. Four legacy citations use the same NASA accession and now resolve to the original presentation. The companion abstract provides no total person-exposure denominator to resolve the 61 daily exposures versus 62 cumulative exposures printed in the slides. Existing EA-6 rows retain that discrepancy. Baker and colleagues report an in-flight F-35C case, and Wrigley reviews published in-flight cases. Both are recorded as literature leads for aviation evidence.
