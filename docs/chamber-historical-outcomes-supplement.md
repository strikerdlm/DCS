# Historical chamber surveillance and clinical outcomes, release 1.24

Release 1.24 adds 16 observation rows, 12 published rate rows, four source records and seven new cohort links. It upgrades the Berry 1958 and Halbouty–Heisler 1955 records using their original pages. The search ledger records 16 operations and 58 screened results or retrievals. [Sources](../data/chamber/v1.24/sources.csv) · [Observations](../data/chamber/v1.24/observations.csv) · [Reported rates](../data/chamber/v1.24/reported_rates.csv) · [Cohort links](../data/chamber/v1.24/overlap.csv) · [Source upgrades](../data/chamber/v1.24/schema.sql) · [Search ledger](../data/chamber/v1.24/search_hits.csv)

- **Berry, USAF, 1958.** The source reports **125 Grade IV chamber reactions requiring hospitalization in 1950–1955**. Table 3 assigns them to five groups of 65, 4, 4, 5 and 47. The separate 1955 table uses **51,580 trainee flights** and reports rates per 100,000. The prose gives an overall Grade IV rate of **85 per 100,000** that year. The Grade IV symptom rates in Table 2 sum to 93 per 100,000. These are kept as separate published measurements, with percentages stored by exact unit conversion. The six-year exposure denominator is unavailable. [1955 rates](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.71421/page/n870/mode/1up) · [Grade IV groups](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.71421/page/n871/mode/1up)
- **Houston, Navy chamber, 1946.** **Six of 20 technicians and medical officers** had symptoms attributed to altitude aero-embolism during acclimatization work. Three reported scotomata and two had unilateral temporal visual loss that persisted after descent. The report gives 387 lock ascents and approximately 15 episodes. Most ascents were made by the 20 staff members. The episode count and denominator are insufficient for an exact per-flight rate. The 1947 journal publication remains linked for likely reuse. [Original summary and reports](https://archive.org/download/DTIC_AD0622164/DTIC_AD0622164.pdf#page=4)
- **Henry, Berkeley chamber, 1953.** **23/375 chamber runs** had source-defined chokes, including 13 mild or incipient and 10 moderate. The report records **6/375 runs with scotomata**. Five of the six also had bends. The outcomes share runs across several experimental protocols, and the author notes early chamber removal when chest symptoms appeared. [Chest symptoms](https://archive.org/download/DTIC_AD0016860/DTIC_AD0016860.pdf#page=50) · [Visual disturbances](https://archive.org/download/DTIC_AD0016860/DTIC_AD0016860.pdf#page=54)
- **Individual clinical reports.** The original Halbouty–Heisler case matches Berry and King's Case 1 and contributes provenance to the existing episode. Schneck's report describes an in-flight case at **24,400 ft cabin pressure altitude** in an aircraft at 40,000 ft. It appears in Berry's selected case table. The case was entered once from Schneck, with no exposure denominator. [Halbouty and Heisler](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.71418/page/n1139/mode/1up) · [Schneck](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.71420/page/n1440/mode/1up)

Firecrawl found the journal archive catalogue and Gottschalk dissertation record. Tavily surfaced the public technical reports. The original archive images and downloaded PDFs supplied the verified counts. The dissertation download returned an empty file, so its record links the constituent papers without adding trial rows.
