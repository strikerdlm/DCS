# Operation Everest I and staff chamber outcomes

The 1946 Navy diary records a July 19 episode of temporary blindness in a member of the operating crew and a July 21 episode of temporary partial blindness in a second observer. The diary calls both cerebral bends. Exact onset, individual pressure histories, staff follow-up and modern clinical adjudication are unavailable. Chamber altitudes for those days remain contextual. The cases have distinct dated records with blank person-exposure denominators. [July 19, PDF p. 34](https://digirepo.nlm.nih.gov/ext/dw/35721900R/PDF/35721900R.pdf#page=34) · [July 21, PDF p. 36](https://digirepo.nlm.nih.gov/ext/dw/35721900R/PDF/35721900R.pdf#page=36)

The diary lists 409 lock ascents. Its last ascent carried the cardiology and X-ray group. The roster of 14 technicians and Houston’s description of a broader 40-person support team provide staffing context. Person-level exposure counts are unavailable. The two case records therefore remain case-conditional. [Final lock ascent, PDF p. 43](https://digirepo.nlm.nih.gov/ext/dw/35721900R/PDF/35721900R.pdf#page=43) · [Houston, American Alpine Journal](https://publications.americanalpineclub.org/articles/12194731100/Operation-Everest-1946)

Houston’s December article describes postdescent observation for three days in the four resident volunteers. The July 26 loss of consciousness and ear block, the July 31 abdominal gas pain and the qualitative mentions of crew bends retain their source labels. These observations supply no additional confirmed DCS cases. [Resident follow-up, p. 1786](https://archive.org/download/NavalMedicalBulletin467121946/NavalMedicalBulletin46-7-121946.pdf#page=873) · [July 26, PDF pp. 38–39](https://digirepo.nlm.nih.gov/ext/dw/35721900R/PDF/35721900R.pdf#page=38) · [July 31, PDF p. 42](https://digirepo.nlm.nih.gov/ext/dw/35721900R/PDF/35721900R.pdf#page=42)

The Navy diary, December article and American Alpine Journal account are three reports of the same experiment. The August *Contact* paper has the same title as the December paper but a separate journal, issue and PMID. Its full text and exact overlap remain unresolved. The Houston and Riley physiology citation is reused under PMID 20251062, with the legacy SciSpace citation assigned to that report using the publisher DOI. These records are separate from the later Operation Everest II and III studies.
