# Chamber case series and symptom onset

Release 1.29 adds 27 observation rows, four published symptom percentages, nine source records and ten source links to the cumulative chamber database. The Tavily and Firecrawl search ledger also includes direct publisher and PubMed checks. [Sources](../data/chamber/v1.29/sources.csv) · [Observations](../data/chamber/v1.29/observations.csv) · [Published percentages](../data/chamber/v1.29/reported_rates.csv) · [Cohort links](../data/chamber/v1.29/overlap.csv) · [22 search operations](../data/chamber/v1.29/search_runs.csv) · [82 hits and targeted checks](../data/chamber/v1.29/search_hits.csv)

| Original source | Findings entered |
| --- | --- |
| Fitzpatrick, 1994 | The referral review gives 38 visual cases among 143 treated altitude-induced neurologic Type II DCS cases. Of the 38, 33 followed chamber exposure, including nine inside observers. All five in-flight cases began at altitude, which allows the chamber-only onset breakdown to be recovered exactly from Table II. The 33 chamber cases include 12 at altitude, nine in the first hour, eight at 1–6 h, two at 6–24 h, one at 32 h and one with unknown onset. [Results and Table II](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/65/8/article-p736.pdf#page=2) |
| Rudge, 1995 | Fourteen of 15 treated pulmonary DCS cases followed chamber exposure. Seven were training cases and seven research cases. Nine first had symptoms at altitude and five at ground level after the chamber exposure. One chamber case had the full chest pain, cough and dyspnea triad. These counts are conditional on treatment. [Results and Table I](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/66/12/article-p1185.pdf#page=2) |
| Reddick, 1978 | Six selected DCS cases followed Fort Rucker Army chamber training. The primary series identifies three inside observers, two pilots and one student. It classifies five cases as pain-only bends with cutaneous manifestations and one as CNS DCS. For the CNS case, symptoms during transfer and subsequent recurrence belong to the same case. [Methods and cases](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/49/10/article-p1229.pdf) |
| Yoneda, 1998 | The author concluded that the cadet probably had cutaneous bends with atopic dermatitis. Symptoms began near 30,000 ft and resolved on descent, with no recurrence reported over 24 hours. The reported 38,000-ft high point applies to the other trainees. [Case report](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/69/7/article-p693.pdf) |
| Steigleman et al., 2003 | A Navy student experienced retro-orbital pain on descent after nine minutes at 25,000 ft. Pain resolved, then recurred with visual disturbance 21 days after exposure. The authors considered possible recurrent DCS and discussed sinusitis. [Case report](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/74/9/article-p985.pdf) |
| Öztürk et al., 2004 | A Turkish pilot candidate spent about 15 minutes at 35,000 ft during a chamber session of about an hour and was transferred for chest pain and dyspnea about two hours after training. The authors attributed the cardiac presentation to coronary gas embolism on clinical grounds. [Case report](https://anatoljcardiol.com/article/AJC-07823/pdf) |
| Ríos-Tejada et al., 1997 and 1998 | The 1997 authors diagnosed presumptive arterial gas embolism. The reported peak was 30,000 ft with 30 minutes of oxygen prebreathe. The abstract places onset at 28,000 ft, while the case narrative places it after resuming oxygen following the 25,000-ft hypoxia exercise. The onset altitude is unresolved. The 1998 correspondence remains an indexed lead pending full text. [Case report](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/68/11/article-p1025.pdf) · [1998 letter](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9810543/) |
| Iseev et al., 1988 | The primary abstract reports 130 people and more than 2,400 chamber ascents. Ryles and Pilmanis report 250 cases and four symptom percentages in their comparison table. Their citation matches the original by authors, topic, journal, volume, issue and pages but prints 1986. The database records the secondary case count and percentages, with the exposure denominator left blank. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3361829/) · [Secondary comparison table](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/67/10/article-p983.pdf#page=4) |

These additions describe selected cases, referral populations and a secondary source count. The U.S. referral cohorts have overlapping collection windows. The Japanese case may fall within historical national surveillance, and the Spanish case and letter have unresolved linkage. The release adds no independent incidence denominator and keeps the locked external evaluation intact.
