| Source | Evidence entered |
| --- | --- |
| Ryles and Pilmanis, 1996 | The Armstrong Laboratory archive reports **447 DCS cases in 1,132 chamber exposures** from October 1983 to June 1994. Its separate female exposure row is 4/147. The clinical breakdown includes 372 joint-pain cases, 53 with paresthesia, 14 with at least one respiratory feature and two with the full chokes triad. Tables and body text give exact clinical counts that use different definitions or values from some abstract percentages. The authors' general statement about repetition differs from the examples in Table III, so the exposure denominator retains its original unit. The archive totals are held for likely overlap with the larger AFRL collection. [Original Results and Tables I–IV](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/67/10/article-p983.pdf#page=2) |
| Gray et al., 1946 | The original lists **852 forced descents in 2,920 flights**. The paper also prints 34.3%, which conflicts with the two counts and is recorded separately. Symptoms among the 852 descents include bends, chokes and gas pain. The ten symptom rows can overlap, and the overall endpoint includes descents from causes other than DCS. [Original Table I](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/joam/17/4/article-p333.pdf#page=2) |
| Henry, 1946 | Series A reports paired case counts for 57 men, including 36 symptomatic on both runs, 10 on the first only, four on the second only and seven on neither. The same source reports separate paired incapacitation counts. Ten series contribute reported percentages and documented exposures for the authors' three casualty definitions. The A* row uses the same Series A exposures through 180 minutes. These definitions and denominators remain distinct from modern DCS categories. [Original methods, Results p. 34 and Table V](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/joam/17/1/article-p28.pdf) |
| Kumar et al., 1992 | The source reports 15 pain-only DCS exposures in 78 crossover runs. Of 39 participants, four were symptomatic in the control arm only and five in the prior-exercise arm only. The total therefore implies three symptomatic in both arms, giving **7/39 control** and **8/39 prior exercise**. An additional VGE row retains the published 39/78 pooled detections. The arms share participants, and the result remains held for cohort linkage to the legacy compilations. [Original Results and Table II](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/63/10/article-p899.pdf#page=4) |
| Rudge, 1992 | The chamber treatment and consultation series is conditional on case ascertainment. Of 221 Type I cases, 104 first presented at altitude and 117 after descent. All 117 ground-onset cases were in trainees or attendants. No denominator for all exposed trainees or attendants is available. The 56 Type I research cases likely overlap the Armstrong series. [Original Tables I–II](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/63/12/article-p1102.pdf#page=2) |
| Schirmer and Workman, 1992 | Two of 510 eligible survey respondents developed DCS after completing their survey cards. Their exclusion yields the 508 responses used in the questionnaire analysis. The denominator describes voluntary respondents, with no verified surveillance window or rate for all trained personnel. [Original Methods](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/63/7/article-p616.pdf#page=2) |
| Brooks, 1984 | The Canadian operational series reports 47 serious cabin pressure-loss incidents and two DCS cases. The original contains conflicting descriptions of the affected aircrew role in Case 24. Case 25 reports a cabin altitude of 30,000 ft and a Type I diagnosis. The database preserves incident and case counts without estimating the number of crew exposures. [Original Canadian Forces report](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/asem/55/12/article-p1154.pdf) |
