# Wartime chamber outcomes from the June 1944 CAM minutes

The National Library of Medicine holds the original minutes of the fourteenth meeting of the Committee on Aviation Medicine’s Subcommittee on Decompression-Sickness at Berkeley on June 26–27, 1944. The minutes are in the John F. Fulton Papers, box 11, folder 16. The v1.57 supplement records two reported outcome counts and 11 supporting measurements. All carry an overlap hold. [Original title page](https://digirepo.nlm.nih.gov/ext/mm/101085500X796/PDF/101085500X796.pdf#page=7) · [NLM finding aid](https://findingaids.nlm.nih.gov/repositories/4/archival_objects/61477)

- Bridge reported 132 cases of “chokes” among 329 participants who made 719 mixed chamber flights. The flight count is the exposure denominator. The relationship to the 1944 167-run Berkeley publication and the 1945 journal paper on chokes remains unresolved at the run level. The minutes also describe a 111-minute oxygen comparison without giving its arm counts. [Minutes, item 3](https://digirepo.nlm.nih.gov/ext/mm/101085500X796/PDF/101085500X796.pdf#page=10) · [Bridge et al. 1944](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/joam/15/5/article-p316.pdf) · [Bridge et al. 1945](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-22-3-398)
- Major Goggio described a Santa Ana frequency of one episode of severe chokes per 1,000 runs of 10 to 60 minutes. The actual event and exposure totals are unavailable. Dr. Blankenhorn reported six episodes of postflight circulatory collapse without a denominator or observation window. These are distinct source-defined findings. [Minutes, pp. 330–332](https://digirepo.nlm.nih.gov/ext/mm/101085500X796/PDF/101085500X796.pdf#page=10)
- Williams and Thomas reported 35 imaging observations from 27 selected people with knee bends. The displayed symptomatic-knee component totals are 46 and 37, while the printed combined total is 63. The database preserves all three printed values and records the arithmetic discrepancy. These radiographic bubble counts and scores use their original measurement units. The later article confirms paired-knee radiography at 35,000 and 38,000 ft. Exact cohort linkage remains unresolved. [Minutes, item 4](https://digirepo.nlm.nih.gov/ext/mm/101085500X796/PDF/101085500X796.pdf#page=11) · [Thomas and Williams](https://doi.org/10.1148/44.3.259)

The archived case presentation and Lund et al.’s later 38,000-ft case report remain a possible match. No new unique case is counted. The November 1944 Air Surgeon’s Bulletin chamber/flight comparison also shares five authors and its overall purpose with the 1945 Liberator study. The original bulletin pages are still needed to reconcile their outcomes. [June case presentation](https://digirepo.nlm.nih.gov/ext/mm/101085500X796/PDF/101085500X796.pdf#page=14) · [Lund et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21016036/) · [Berkeley bibliography, p. 134](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/record/54360/files/nuclearmedicine00lawrrich.pdf?ln=en)
