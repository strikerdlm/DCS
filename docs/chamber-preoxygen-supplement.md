# Henry and Cook chamber preoxygenation tables

The v1.56 supplement records Henry and Cook’s three Berkeley chamber experiments in `publication_preoxygen_group` and `publication_preoxygen_measure`. Sixteen table groups contain 83 printed values and one explicit blank. The source distinguishes any symptoms, symptoms reaching grade 3 on the California scale, and incapacitation leading to early descent. The clinical benchmark continues to use its frozen eligibility criteria. [Original study, pp. 350–355](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/joam/16/5/article-p350.pdf) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21005555/)

- **Table I** reports rates and average times at 38,000 ft. The 75 control participants made repeated ascents, and the final number of eligible control ascents is unavailable. The 61 preoxygenated participants made one ascent per condition. The source reports 14 incapacitations in the two-hour condition. Its one-hour label and printed 15-minute advance relative to the control are both retained. The two-hour exposure allowed an additional 20 minutes at altitude. The paper reports no new onset, substantial worsening or descent after minute 90. [Methods and Table I, pp. 350–351](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/joam/16/5/article-p350.pdf#page=2)
- **Table II** reports participant-weighted rates at 35,000 ft for the preoxygenated runs. The comparison rates came from interpolation of other runs. The authors adjusted the primary controls for age and time of day and reported an unweighted all-run reference alongside them. The printed subgroup sizes of 48 and 29 are retained with their counting unit unresolved. [Table II and methods, p. 352](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/joam/16/5/article-p350.pdf#page=3)
- **Table III** reports two ascents per condition for 12 selected participants. Time begins with exercise on ascent at 25,000 ft during a climb to 35,000 ft. The four-hour incapacitation row reports 0%, and its mean time of incapacitation is blank. [Table III and methods, p. 353](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/joam/16/5/article-p350.pdf#page=4)

Henry’s 1947 paper cites the 1945 study for its calculated comparison rates. The three unweighted Table II rates of 82.8%, 66.0% and 53.3% match all three 100%-selected controls in the 1947 Table III. The citation database links the two reports through this documented reuse. The identity of the preoxygenated runs remains unresolved. The 1945 authors also cite Bridge’s 38,000-ft study and Cook’s earlier comparison of exercise and altitude. Their reports share methods and investigators. Exact run linkage remains unavailable.
